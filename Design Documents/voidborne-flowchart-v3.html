VOIDBORNE — DESIGN OVERVIEW

A modded-Minecraft sandbox with guns, vehicles, archetypes, and a tragic story about a corrupted world. This page collects every system we've discussed so each one is easy to find when implementing.

1. Core Vision

Modded Minecraft, but with smooth marching-cubes terrain, guns and ranged combat, modular vehicles, a main story quest, and short repeatable gameplay loops that always make progress feel earned. Stardew Valley is the spiritual sibling: cozy at the surface, deep when you commit.

The world's defining tragedy: the Kin were once a thriving people. Most have been corrupted by an entity called VORD. The player is one of the few Untouched, awakening into the ruin. Reclamation is the spine of every gameplay system.

Smooth Terrain + Grid Building — the design tension

The world uses smooth marching-cubes terrain. Buildings use a 1×1 grid (Minecraft-style). Reconciliation:

  • The grid is player-asserted. When you place your first block, the world locks a virtual grid origin at that point. All future placements snap to it.
  • Terrain leveling tools let you carve smooth terrain into flat grid platforms. You're not fighting the terrain — you're shaping it.
  • Mining smooth terrain creates smooth caves; mining grid-placed blocks removes them cleanly. Two systems coexist visibly.

3. Building System

Every building material has 5 forms: cube, slab, panel, stairs, door. ~17 materials × 5 forms = 85+ blocks. Plus 45+ decorative blocks themed per archetype.

Enemy and NPC structures use the same grid system, so VORD scrap and Kin remnants give you enemy-themed building blocks. Players can rebuild captured strongholds in their own style.

Implementation Note Building blocks snap to a virtual grid that's offset from the world origin per-player on first placement. Pre-placed Kin ruins and Vord strongholds use the same grid but with their own offset, visually integrating into the smooth terrain at place-time.

5. Power System

Power generation is its own progression layer. More power = faster production via the Overclock Module. Higher-tier machines refuse to run on undersized cables.

Cable TierCapacityUse
T1≤200WBasic conveyors, lighting
T2≤1000WAuto-machines, robotic arms
T3≤5000WEndgame factories

Power management items: Junction Box, Voltage Regulator (prevents surge damage), Battery Pack (storage), Overclock Module (2× power → 2-3× speed), Power Sink (burns excess).

Each archetype has its own power gen flavor: solar (tech), pedal/treadmill (bio), beehive (beekeeper), tidal/wind (mechanical), spore bioreactor (alchemist), eel oil lamp (bestmaster), and many more.

Wheels & locomotion: Wood, Iron, Spiked, Rubber, Studded, Bone, Pneumatic, Mag-Wheel, Iron Tracks, Spike Tracks, Skis, Hover Pad, Mech Legs, Helicopter Rotor, Plane Prop, Void Thruster.

8. Combat & Skill Asymmetry

Solo is the primary experience. Co-op adds flavor. The asymmetric-skill problem (one player is much better at twitch combat than another) is solved via multiple paths to victory per boss:

  • A boss might have a frontal weak point (skilled aim)
  • A glowing weak point you can shoot from cover (less skill)
  • A vulnerability to poisoned bait (alchemist trap)
  • A timing window where heavy defenses (turrets, traps) can carry you

Solo player picks one path. Co-op covers multiple at once for faster kills. No mechanic requires two players.

10. Spirit Gateway System

The emotional and mechanical backbone. Every boss was once a Kin, corrupted by Vord. Defeating + cleansing them releases their spirit, which can take up residence in your base.

Three-item flow:

  • Spirit Anchor — small portable item dropped by the cleansed boss.
  • Spirit Gateway — placeable block in your base. Holds the spirit. Click to summon, dismiss, or assign tasks.
  • Spirit Echo — the manifested ghost, wanders your base freely. Invisible to enemies.

What spirits do:

  • Unlock unique masterwork-tier recipes only available from them.
  • Passively boost a related machine in your base (Chef spirit speeds up your nearest oven, etc).
  • Provide cryptic hints about other bosses ("She fears the cold but loves the dance.").
  • Offer side quests that deepen the relationship.

Relationship tiers:

StrangerJust rescued. Basic recipes unlocked.
AcquaintanceUsed a few times. Passive boost activates.
FriendOffered gifts, completed side quest. Masterwork recipe unlocks.
BondedCompleted deep personal quest. Unique mechanic activates.

By endgame, your base houses ~30 spirits, each at varying levels. The base becomes a memorial — every machine has a Kin's story attached to it.

11. Death & Recall

Death is meaningful but not demoralizing. No XP loss; no equipment destruction.

The Recall Box — placeable, cheap, anywhere safe. On death, respawn at the most recent Recall Box. Multiple placements supported (forward outposts).

What you keep on death: all equipped gear (slight durability loss ~10%). Death feels like a setback, not a punishment.

What you lose on death: contents of your inventory (gathered materials, crafted items). They drop where you died.

Three retrieval paths:

Walk BackFree. Items stay where you fell. Time cost.
Recovery BeaconPre-crafted, single-use. Teleports your items home. Cost = beacon materials.
Spirit RetrievalPay a Kin spirit an offering. Convenient, but uses a relationship favor.

Recall Box variants per archetype: Basic, Spirit Recall Stone (mystic), Tech Reconstitution Pod (faster), Living Bramble Refuge (gardener, auto-heals), Mechanical Cradle (clockwork, slower but maintenance-free).

12. Atlas System

The Atlas is the single artifact that visualizes your progression. Crafted only after defeating the first boss, which drops a Cartographer's Lens — that first kill is the unlock for everything else.

Scrollable infinite map. Shows:

  • Cleared strongholds (greyed out)
  • Active strongholds (highlighted)
  • Discovered NPCs (faces in their hometowns)
  • Your bases and Recall Boxes
  • Personal pins/notes
  • Region corruption levels
  • Resource hotspots you've found

Atlas Upgrades (mini-progression):

Paper AtlasBasic map. Player position, manual pins.
Brass Atlas+ Diviner's Compass (reveals hidden strongholds in radius)
Electronic AtlasConnected to base power. Auto-updates. Shows enemy positions in real-time.

How players discover content: Regions of Interest appear as vague shaded zones. NPCs share rumors. Merchants sell location info. Resource trails (Vord scrap density) hint at strongholds. There's always something to head toward.

13. Quest Loop

Bounty Board — placeable in your base. Generates 3 random daily objectives. Completing them drops Reward Caches.

Quest types: Hunt X creature, Deliver Y item, Clear a corruption node, Defend a Kin village, Bring an offering to a spirit. Each is repeatable.

Story quests live in the Atlas as marked story strongholds. Side quests live in the Bounty Board.

Stronghold raids — the dungeon analog. Procedurally placed beyond the initial 30 hand-crafted ones. Tiered:

  • Small Camp (~15 min, light combat)
  • Outpost (~30 min, drops Stronghold Keys, Heart Fragments)
  • Fortress (~45 min, Vord Marrow, Citadel Map Fragments)
  • Citadel (boss raid, prestige drops)

Echo Strongholds — re-fight content. Procedurally generated, found further from the starting region. Modifiers randomize: Mist-shrouded, Swarming, Hardened, Soul-bound. Drop extra materials + cosmetic Echo Materials.

14. Durability — Three Quality Tiers

Items consume but late-game gear feels permanent.

TierDurabilityRepair LimitSource
CrudeShort~5 repairs then breaksBasic crafting
Standard~10× crude~5 repairs then breaksStandard recipes
Masterwork~50× crudeInfiniteBoss-drop recipes via Spirit advisors

Soulbound items — handful of items don't degrade at all. Reanimated Pet, your starter blade, things players form attachments to. Lets emotional items stay safe while functional items consume.

Ammo and consumables always consume — they're the daily crafting loop.

Field Repair Kit — portable, lets you repair mid-mission. Repair is cheap; replacement is the real cost.

15. Corruption & Reclamation

Corruption is the spreading enemy presence — but does not threaten player bases while you're offline. No grief-style spread.

Vord Corruption Nodes are placed in the world. Around each, a slowly-growing corrupted zone. The zone can grow during gameplay sessions but is gated and bounded — never reaches your base unannounced.

Player tools to push back:

  • Purifier Lantern — handheld, cleanses corrupted tiles
  • Cleansing Brazier — area cleanse, slow but powerful
  • Reclamation Beacon — placed after defeating a node, locks the area permanently safe
  • Sanctuary Lattice — building block that resists corruption
  • Anti-Corruption Salve — consumable, cures corrupted status on player

Reclaimed land drops Purified Soil — premium gardener material. Reclaiming a region unlocks new resources, NPCs, and stronghold targets.

16. Festivals & Merchants

Festivals — periodic in-game events (every X days). Festival Lantern, Fireworks, Banquet Table, Maypole only usable during. Festivals provide:

  • Unique food recipes
  • Boosted NPC happiness (faster work)
  • Special Bounty Board quests
  • One-time merchant goods

Merchants — traveling NPCs visit your base if you've placed a Caravan Beacon + Trade Post. Sell rare goods, buy your surplus. Trader's Ledger tracks dealings and unlocks discounts.

17. Ideal Session Shape

A satisfying 45–60 min session, designed top-to-bottom.

  1. Open Atlas — see current state of your world. (1–2 min)
  2. Visit Spirit Gateway — get advice from a relevant Kin spirit. (1–2 min)
  3. Check Bounty Board — see any side quests aligning with your plan. (2 min)
  4. Prep — craft consumables, repair gear, pack a Recovery Beacon, place a forward Recall Box. (5–10 min)
  5. Travel — vehicle or foot to the stronghold, knock out a bounty en route. (5–10 min)
  6. Fight — clear fodder, beat boss. (15–30 min)
  7. Return + Place — boss drop slotted into new machine. Spirit Anchor placed in Gateway. New Kin joins your base. (5 min)
  8. Update Atlas — stronghold marked complete. New regions of interest appear from the rescued Kin's intel. Plan next session. (1 min)

You can stop after any step and still feel productive. Even just step 4 (crafting prep) feels like progress.

VOIDBORNE — STORY OUTLINE

First draft. The 4-act structure mapped to gameplay milestones. This is meant to be torn apart and rebuilt many times — but it gives shape to the world.

The World Before

The Kin were a civilization of artisans, beekeepers, scholars, and sky-walkers, scattered across a marching-cube continent of badlands, marshes, frozen peaks, and floating sky islands. Their cities were small but proud — each region a different cultural flavor. They worshipped no gods but kept the memories of their ancestors in sigil-stones, carved soul-anchors that bound a person's spirit to the world after death.

Then VORD came — or rather, broke through. From an abyssal rift in the deep void, a corrupting force entered the world. Not an army. Not a god. A condition. It spreads through the air, through soil, through the spirits of the recently dead. The Kin were transformed: their bodies remade into Vord drones, their memories shredded but never erased — buried inside the corrupted creature's hollow chest, still pulsing like a heart that won't stop remembering.

By the time the corruption reached its present extent, almost every Kin was gone. The world fell into a long quiet.

ACT 1 — AWAKENING

~5–10 hrs Tutorial layer Builds basic base Rescues first Kin

Opening situation

The player wakes in a sealed stone vault beneath an old Kin sanctuary. They are an Untouched — somehow, the corruption did not take them. They have no memory of who placed them there or why they were spared. The vault was built to last; the world outside has changed beyond recognition.

The vault contains: a simple bedroll, a wooden tool kit, a sealed letter in fading ink ("If you read this, you are the last of us. Find the others."), and a sigil-stone faintly glowing.

Major beats

  1. Exit the vault. Player learns basic movement, hand-gathering, building a Workbench. Above ground: a ruined village, no Kin in sight, only quiet wind and distant glows on the horizon.
  2. First encounter. A small corrupted creature — once a Kin child, by its size — wanders into the camp. Non-aggressive at first. The player can study it, kill it, or scare it away. This is the first emotional gut-punch: the corrupted are still recognizable.
  3. Build a Recall Box. Story-justified tutorial for the death system. The sigil-stone from the vault is the key component — the player learns that sigil-stones hold spirits, and a Recall Box anchors them the same way.
  4. The first stronghold appears. Smoke from a distant ridge. Player investigates, finds a small Vord camp — fodder enemies, a "boss-lite" miniboss (a Fungal Brood Mother variant scaled for early play).
  5. The first rescue. Inside the camp's cage: a single Kin survivor, mute with fear. She doesn't speak yet, but she follows. The player must escort her home. This is the first NPC. The first proof that some Kin remain.

New mechanic introduced

Crafting, building, basic combat, the Recall Box. By act end, the player has a small base, one rescued Kin (acting as a quiet companion), and a single defeated boss's drop.

Boss

The Fungal Brood Mother — Tier 1. A bloated, slow Vord creature in the ruined village basement. Hits hard but predictable. Drops the Cartographer's Lens, which is required to craft the first Atlas.

Closing transition

The rescued Kin — call her Wren — finally speaks at the end of Act 1. She tells the player that there were other survivors. That the Kin Council, before the fall, had begun preparing for this. That somewhere, scattered across the world, are six Anchors — places where the Council bound the spirits of their greatest people. If those Anchors can be reached, the spirits can be released to help. The Atlas reveals five distant regions of interest. The player chooses where to go first.

ACT 2 — RECLAMATION

~20 hrs Explore the biomes Rescue 6 named Kin Unlock most archetypes

Opening situation

The player has the Atlas and a small base. The five regions are open: Lowlands, Badlands, Frozen Peaks, Fungal Marshes, and the Sky Islands. Each region has its own corruption nodes, hidden Kin survivors, and a major stronghold guarding an Anchor.

Wren stays at the base, gradually opening up as the player rescues more Kin. She remembers fragments — old recipes, half-songs, the names of those who used to live in each region.

Major beats

  1. The Lowlands. Easiest region. Player meets traveling traders (the first non-Kin NPCs — descendants of pre-Kin nomads who survived corruption by staying mobile). They sell information about distant strongholds. Boss: The Stone Warden (Tier 1 Warden family). Drops a smith's hammer required for the Forge.
  2. The Fungal Marshes. Spore-thick, vision limited. Player encounters the first Channeler family boss — the Mire Channeler. Drops alchemist materials. Reclaiming the marsh's heart reveals Kin survivor #2, a former apothecary who teaches the player the Chemistry Set recipe.
  3. The Badlands. Hot, sparse, dotted with old Kin smithing forges. The Pyro archetype awakens here. Boss: The Cinder Caster. The freed survivor is a smith who knew Wren — a moment of recognition between two Kin who thought they were alone.
  4. The Frozen Peaks. Brutal cold. The Cryo archetype unlocks. The boss here is a Frost Hunter (Tier 2) — fast, hard to track in snow. The survivor is a hunter herself, who tells the player about the Sky Islands and the original VORD intrusion point.
  5. The Sky Islands. First aerial gameplay. The Glider Frame is required to even reach them (player must complete a Lowlands stronghold to get the recipe). Boss: The Sky Hunter. The survivor here is a Kin scholar who reveals the truth: Vord is not an invader. Vord is a wound in the world. Something broke through.

Sub-loop: Spirit Gateway built

Around the middle of Act 2, the player gathers enough Anchors and Soul Essences to construct the first Spirit Gateway. The first spirit to manifest is the apothecary — they begin advising on alchemist recipes. From this point forward, every cleansed boss drops a Spirit Anchor that can be placed in the Gateway, growing the base's population.

Closing transition

By the end of Act 2, the player has rescued ~6 Kin, defeated ~8 bosses (mix of T1 and T2), unlocked most archetype recipe families, and revealed the location of the Citadel — the central Vord fortress at the original rift, where the Heart of the Hive resides. But the Citadel cannot be entered: it is sealed by a barrier woven from seven Heart Fragments, each held by a Vord Aberrant scattered across the world. The fragments are not just keys — they are pieces of the original wound. Recovering them is what closes the rift.

ACT 3 — THE HEART FRAGMENTS

~20 hrs Hunt 7 Aberrants Deep automation begins Difficulty ramps hard

Opening situation

The player's base is thriving — multiple NPCs, several spirits in the Gateway, vehicles, defenses. But the easy bosses are done. What remains is the Aberrant family: seven unique, multi-phase nightmares, each holding a Heart Fragment. They are not simple corrupted Kin. They are amalgamations — the rift's attempt to make something new.

Major beats

  1. The Tendril Horror — a many-limbed thing buried in a sunken Kin temple in the marshes. Reaches through walls. Forces the player to manage line-of-sight and traps. Drops the first Heart Fragment + reveals: the Kin Council knew the rift was coming. They prepared. They failed.
  2. The Echoing Heart — a Vord boss that fights using copies of itself. Cleansing it requires identifying the "real" one. The player must learn to listen for the rhythm of the real Heart's pulse beneath the chorus of fakes.
  3. The Hollow Choir — sky island, multi-vocal entity. The "boss" is six separate creatures that sing together; they must be killed in the right harmonic order or the others heal. Drops a Heart Fragment + a Kin Diary that says: the Choir were our singers. We did this to ourselves trying to wake them.
  4. The Maw of the Abyss — deep cave system. The boss is a gravity well. The player fights while being pulled toward an event horizon. Drops a Heart Fragment + the first hint that the rift is not a wound but a door.
  5. The Forgotten King — a Kin monarch, preserved in the moment of corruption, ruling over a stronghold of his own. He is dignified. He speaks. He asks the player questions about why they are doing this. He fights only when convinced the player will not stop. This is the moral hinge of Act 3 — the King's spirit, when freed, becomes the most powerful Gateway resident, but also the one who delivers the harshest truths about what the player is choosing.
  6. The Carapace Apostle — a final corrupted Kin who had become a Vord priest. Voluntarily. Worships the rift. The player must decide whether to free their spirit or let them remain. Either choice has consequences for Act 4.
  7. The seventh Fragment is missing. The player must figure out where it went. Investigation reveals: Wren, your first rescued Kin, has been carrying it inside her this entire time. She is also dying because of it. The player must decide: take the Fragment and end her, or leave it and lose the path to the Citadel.

New mechanic introduced

Heavy automation. By Act 3, the player needs to be mass-producing ammo, food, and prep materials to survive the Aberrant fights. The factory begins to feel necessary.

Spirit relationships also deepen. Spirits start to give long-form personal quests that take real time to complete. Some refuse to help if the player makes certain choices.

Closing transition

The player has all 7 Heart Fragments, or has made a terrible choice about Wren. The Citadel barrier is open. The Atlas updates: a single, massive marker has appeared in the center of the map, where it has always been waiting.

ACT 4 — THE SOURCE

~5 hrs The Citadel Final choice Near-impossible without prep

Opening situation

The Citadel is not what the player expected. It is not a fortress; it is the original Kin Council Hall, rebuilt by Vord around the rift. Inside, every wall is a memory. Spirits walk the halls — not free, but bound. The Vord has been preserving the Kin all this time, in its own way. Not destroying them. Holding them.

Major beats

  1. The Hollow Source — first major fight. A vast Vord creature woven from every kind of Kin: smith, beekeeper, dancer, scholar. It cycles through their attack patterns. The player must use everything they've learned about every boss family. The fight is genuinely hard. Players who skipped archetypes will struggle here.
  2. The Choice Chamber. Beyond the Hollow Source, the rift itself. A wound, a door, a presence. The player can:
    • Seal it. Use all 7 Fragments to close the rift. Vord ends. The corrupted die — including all the Kin still inside them. The world is safe but quiet.
    • Bind it. Use the Fragments to contain the rift instead of closing it. Vord remains, weakened. The corrupted can be slowly cleansed, one at a time, over years. Hope but burden.
    • Step Through. Take the Fragments through the rift yourself. The world is left without you, but without Vord either. Whatever is on the other side, you will face. The Spirit Gateway in your base will keep your friends safe.
    • Embrace. Become Vord. The corruption was a wound, yes — but also a tool. The player who chooses this becomes the next Source. The world is yours to shape. (This ending unlocks New Game+ as a corrupting force, playing the world from the other side.)
  3. The Final Boss — depends on the choice. Each choice has its own confrontation:
    • Seal → the rift itself, as an entity
    • Bind → the Council's hidden Apostle, who opposes binding
    • Step Through → no boss; an extended traversal sequence into the unknown
    • Embrace → the rejected Untouched, the one who would not corrupt — yourself, in mirror

Closing

The world resolves based on the choice. The player's base remains — and is now visited by the spirits they freed, the Kin they saved, the traders they befriended. The game continues as a sandbox in the resolved world. Echo Strongholds keep spawning beyond the starting region. New procedural challenges appear. Some players will replay; some will live in the world they earned.

Endgame & Replay

New Game+ retains the player's Atlas knowledge, their Spirit Gateway (now a memorial only — the spirits stay with you), and unlocks harder difficulty modifiers. The "Embrace" ending unlocks a fundamentally different game mode where the player spreads corruption instead of cleansing it.

Echo Strongholds continue to appear in the wider world even after the main story ends. Players can keep hunting Vord remnants for masterwork upgrades and rare cosmetics indefinitely.

Story Themes — Recurring Beats

  • The corrupted are still recognizable. Every Vord enemy was once someone. Memory Echoes always remind the player.
  • You cannot save everyone. Some Kin are too far gone. Some choose corruption. The player must accept loss.
  • The world keeps going. NPCs have their own lives, festivals happen with or without you, the merchants travel routes even when you don't see them.
  • Kindness is a strategy. Diplomats can talk to corrupted Kin in lucid moments. Pacifists save lives without violence. Skilled combat is one path of many.
